# Idea lenses

Run each lens against the platform. A lens that yields nothing for this platform is skipped, not forced.

## Follow the money already moving

- What do creators or developers on this platform already pay other people for? Check freelance listings, "hire a ..." posts, commission threads, and asset or template marketplaces. Repeated paid requests are demand you can see.
- What do they pay platforms or tools for, and which of those tools are disliked, expensive or missing a feature people request?
- What do brands or studios pay for here (sponsorships, ads, custom work), and who does the matching today?

## Look for pain in people's own words

- Recurring complaints in subreddits, forums, creator Discords and tool reviews. Quote the wording; it becomes the pitch.
- Tasks people describe as tedious, manual or done in a spreadsheet.
- "Is there a tool for ..." questions with no good answer, or answers that all name the same unhappy option.

## Look at change

- Recent platform features, API releases, policy or payout changes. Each one creates a short window where existing tools are missing.
- Rising categories the tooling has not caught up with.
- Anything the platform stopped supporting, which strands users.

## Look at who is underserved

- A segment the main tools ignore: a language, a country, an age group, a small niche, a disability, a professional use.
- Beginners who bounce off the current tools, or power users who outgrew them.

## Move things between places

- A workflow that works on one platform and has no equivalent on another.
- Data the platform holds but does not show (analytics, trends, benchmarks) that creators would pay to see, where the platform's terms allow collecting it.
- Combining two audiences that do not overlap yet.

## Sort each candidate

Tag every idea native, picks-and-shovels or arbitrage. If nearly all your list is native, go back and run the money and pain lenses again aimed at the people who earn on the platform.
